PET bottles are among the easiest plastics to recycle, and also among the easiest to spoil. A dry, colour-sorted, cap-free lot is a clean material. A wet bag of mixed bottles with caps, labels and leftover liquid is something a buyer has to clean up before it can be used. This guide covers how to prepare PET scrap so it holds its value.

Where PET bottle scrap comes from

Beverage and water bottling units, pharmaceutical and syrup packaging lines, hotels and restaurants, event organisers, offices, canteens, institutions and housing societies all generate PET scrap. The volumes differ, but the sorting rules are the same.

Step one: sort by colour

Clear / transparent
Light blue
Green
Mixed / dark colours

Clear PET is generally the most flexible material for recyclers, so keeping it separate from coloured bottles is the single most useful thing you can do. Light blue is often handled with clear. Green and darker colours go in their own lots.

Step two: prepare the bottles

  1. Empty them completely. Liquid, syrup and sugar residue attract dirt and make a lot heavier without adding value.
  2. Remove caps and rings where practical. Caps are usually a different plastic from the bottle.
  3. Peel off labels if you can. Shrink sleeves and paper labels count as contamination.
  4. Crush and compact. Flattened bottles take far less space and are cheaper to move.
  5. Bale or bag, and keep dry. Strapped bales or sealed bags, stored under cover and off the floor.

PET, HDPE or PVC? A quick way to check

PlasticUsually looks likeMarking
PETClear or lightly coloured, rigid, crackles when squeezed (water and soft drink bottles)1
HDPEOpaque or milky, waxy feel (milk, detergent and shampoo bottles)2
PVCOften stiff with a bluish or cloudy tint, and may whiten when bent3

Check the marking on the base when you are unsure. Keep the three apart, because PVC mixed into PET is a particular problem for recyclers.

Common mistakes that lower the value

Mixing colours. One bag of everything is graded as the lowest category present.
Leaving caps, labels and liquids in. They add weight that is not PET and require extra cleaning.
Mixing in other plastics. HDPE jars, PVC bottles and oil containers do not belong in a PET lot.
Storing in the rain. Wet bales are heavier, harder to handle and may be marked down.
Bottles that held chemicals or oil. Keep these completely apart from food and drink bottles.
